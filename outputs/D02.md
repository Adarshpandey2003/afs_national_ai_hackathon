### NIR diffuse reflection (Diffuse reflection fiber-optic probe; reflectance-to-absorbance, MSC or SNV, then PLS regression for fat and protein): primary [S01 p.17; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- NIR reflection is primarily used for opaque or highly concentrated dairy products such as yogurt, butter, or whole milk concentrates, and the source describes PLS regression to quantify fat and protein. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17]

- A diffuse reflection probe is listed for scattering media with 0.5-1 mm optical depth and average sensitivity, selectivity, and cost. *(sourced)* [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

### NIR transmission or transflection (Transmission or transflection fiber probe with 1-10 mm optical path): comparison [S01 p.18; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- NIR transmission is preferred for transparent or semi-transparent liquids and may offer higher accuracy for low-concentration components because light interacts with the whole sampled cross-section. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18]

- Transmission fails when the milk becomes too thick or fat-rich; a transflection probe may work for low-fat and high-fat containing milk products. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18]

- The same NIR spectrometer and light source remain, making a reflection-versus-transmission/transfection comparison experimentally cheap. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18]

Applies if: Only if the opaque product still permits a useful optical path.; Pathlength must be precisely controlled and confirmed on the real product.

### MIR ATR (ATR immersion probe with mid-infrared fiber; contact evanescent-wave measurement): comparison [S01 p.18; S01 Table 1 (p.6); S04 p.3]

- Advanced dairy labs incorporate MIR ATR fiber-optic techniques to capture specific chemical information that NIR might miss, giving higher molecular specificity. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18; S01 p.10]

- The ATR path is only 1-6 um, so it can measure highly scattering or opaque fluids without dilution. *(sourced)* [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- S04 identifies triglyceride carbonyl near 1740 cm-1 and protein amide I/II bands near 1548 and 1640 cm-1, but warns that in inhomogeneous fat-rich samples lipid and protein signatures mix, reducing classification usefulness. *(sourced)* [S04 p.3]

Applies if: Only if NIR selectivity or robustness is insufficient for fat/protein.; Contact, fouling, crystal compatibility, and usable fiber/crystal range must be confirmed.; Higher cost and more demanding probe integration are acceptable.

## Risks and uncertainty

- The S01 dairy statements are vendor application examples, not validated performance for this customer's concentrated product; no accuracy or detection limits are given. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17]

- NIR reflection is sensitive to surface characteristics and fat globule size, so texture variation could mimic composition change despite MSC/SNV preprocessing. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17]

- Temperature effects on dairy spectra are not covered by AFS sources and must be treated as an open experimental variable. *(inference)*

- MIR ATR analyses only the crystal tip region and has contact/fouling limitations; no dairy cleaning or fouling-rate data are supplied. *(sourced)* [S04 p.3]

- A reflection or ATR spectrum is not itself a fat or protein calibration; a matrix-specific chemometric model with thorough validation is required. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17; S03 p.6; S03 p.14]

## Feasibility plan

**Objective:** Determine whether NIR diffuse reflection, alone or compared with NIR transmission/transfection and MIR ATR, can provide earlier fat/protein information under real temperature and texture variation, using retained laboratory checks as reference.

**Samples**

- Representative production batches across the expected fat and protein ranges and texture states.

- Samples taken at the intended measurement point, including low, mid, and high temperature/texture conditions as available.

**Reference measurements**

- Existing laboratory fat and protein reference assays with documented sampling time and uncertainty.

- Any additional target variables, if the client confirms them later, should have independent reference values.

1. **Laboratory screening of candidate methods**: Record spectra on representative retained samples using NIR diffuse reflection, NIR transmission/transfection, and MIR ATR probes. Record sample temperature and texture at each measurement and identify the usable spectral window for each method. [S02 p.3; S01 p.17; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

2. **Build first chemometric models**: Build fat/protein PLS models for each viable method against retained laboratory reference values. Apply MSC or SNV for NIR reflection and include temperature/texture as variability. Use segmented and random-subset validation to avoid overfitting. [S01 p.17; S03 p.6; S03 p.14]

3. **In-line or in-situ trial**: Move the best laboratory candidate to the agreed process point. Take timed reference samples and compare model predictions with laboratory results while checking robustness to temperature, texture, and any fouling or cleaning effects. [S01 p.17; S05 p.1; S05 p.2]

4. **Data fusion comparison**: Only if no single method meets the agreed requirements, test combined NIR reflection plus MIR ATR or other chosen methods with aligned sampling location and timing. A combination must beat the best single method. [S01 p.7; S01 p.8]

5. **Outlook to a simplified sensor**: If a model works, identify the key informative wavelengths and assess whether a simplified OMS or LED-based multi-wavelength sensor could replace the full-spectrum instrument for routine use. [S01 p.9; S03 p.14]

**Comparison:** Compare single-method prediction error, bias, and robustness on independent samples against client-agreed fat and protein thresholds. Choose the simplest configuration that meets them; consider data fusion only if it beats the best single method.

**Go / no-go:** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client before the test, such as fat/protein prediction error, bias, response delay, and robustness to temperature and texture. The go/no-go decision is made against those agreed thresholds; no acceptance numbers are invented here.
