## Feasibility plan

**Objective:** Determine whether one or more fiber-optic methods can detect, and preferably quantify, the target melt composition change against offline assays before choosing a probe.

**Samples**

- Representative melt samples spanning the client-agreed target composition change and normal production variation.

- Samples representing relevant physical variation, such as temperature, pressure, melt homogeneity and cooling/history.

- Interface or fouling challenge samples or aged probe windows, if build-up is expected.

- Independent process samples collected at timed intervals during the in-line trial.

**Reference measurements**

- Client's named offline assay(s) for the target component, with description of measurand, sampling time and reported uncertainty.

- Timed in-line sample collection paired to optical acquisitions.

- Independent reference method for calibration, such as chromatographic or spectrophotometric assay, to be confirmed with the client; no specific method is assumed from the intake.

1. **Lab screening of candidate methods**: Obtain characteristic spectra of representative melt samples with MIR-ATR, NIR and Raman, plus UV-Vis if a suitable chromophore exists, to identify usable spectral windows and whether the target change is visible at likely concentration levels. [S01 p.4; S02 p.20]

2. **Reference assay pairing and calibration**: Build chemometric calibration or classification models against the independent reference results; use segmented validation so repeated measurements from the same sample or position are excluded together. [S01 p.12; S03 p.6; S03 p.13]

3. **Single-method comparison before fusion**: Compare single-method model error, bias and robustness; test NIR/Raman or MIR/fluorescence fusion only if a single method is insufficient or complementary bands are needed. [S01 p.7; S01 p.15]

4. **In-line trial at real measurement point**: Install the candidate probe at the agreed extruder location; collect timed reference samples while varying normal process conditions and observing fouling or cleaning behaviour. [S02 p.14; S01 p.10]

5. **Simplification review**: If a model meets acceptance, identify the key wavelengths or features that a simplified dedicated multi-wavelength or IoT sensor could use; otherwise return to lab screening with a revised interface or method. [S02 p.12; S02 p.20; S05 p.1]

**Comparison:** Compare methods on calibration or prediction error, bias, ability to track composition change, robustness to fouling and process variation; prefer the simplest single method that meets client acceptance.

**Go / no-go:** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the client before the test. No go/no-go numbers are invented here; choose a method only if it meets the agreed accuracy and robustness on independent samples.
