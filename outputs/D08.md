## Measurement concept

**Probe geometry**

- A simultaneous combined probe uses an ATR crystal tip in contact with the tissue for MIR measurement; additional silica fibers connected to the base of the crystal transmit UV-visible light for fluorescence measurement at the same point. *(sourced)* [S01 p.8; S04 p.3]

- Sample contact is limited to the flat ATR crystal tip; the MIR ATR channel samples a surface layer of a few microns, while fluorescence samples a deeper tissue volume. *(sourced)* [S01 p.13; S04 p.3]

**Location**

- Measure in a controlled laboratory setup on excised model tissues. If comparing separate single-method measurements, use a position-coding grid and consistent distributed points; if using the simultaneous combined probe, the same point is measured by both methods. *(inference)* [S03 p.3; S04 p.2; S04 p.3]

**Fiber link**

- The MIR channel can use chalcogenide As-S IR fiber; its transmission covers approximately 1.1–6.5 µm, not the entire nominal MIR range, but it includes the cited diagnostic tissue bands at 1740, 1640 and 1548 cm−1. *(sourced)* [S02 p.6; S04 p.3]

## Risks and uncertainty

- Single methods may be insufficient: in S04's PCA, IR separated fat best and fluorescence separated liver best, but neither method alone completely separated all three tissue classes. *(sourced)* [S04 p.4]

- Tissue heterogeneity affects reproducibility: fat showed low MIR reproducibility because spectra always represent a mixture of lipids and neighboring proteins, which can weaken classification based on protein bands. *(sourced)* [S04 p.3]

- Sequential grid measurements cannot guarantee complete coincidence of the spots analyzed by both methods, so fused-data conclusions from sequential measurements may not reflect true same-point information. *(sourced)* [S04 p.2]

- This is a laboratory technical-note result only; S04 is not evidence of process or clinical readiness, so no clinical, diagnostic or in-line conclusions should be drawn from the first experiment. *(sourced)* [S04 p.3; S01 p.9]

- Data fusion can be dominated by differences in variance, noise and variable count between methods; the fusion gain must be tested with appropriate validation rather than assumed from exploratory PCA alone. *(sourced)* [S01 p.9; S04 p.4]

**Comparison:** Compare IR-only, fluorescence-only and fused IR+fluorescence models on identical samples; also compare a two-variable rule such as IR absorbance near 1743 cm−1 plus fluorescence near 613 nm against each single variable.

**Go / no-go:** Agree with the client in advance what separation or validation improvement is meaningful. If fused same-point data separates all three classes on independent held-out samples or points and outperforms both single methods, proceed to expanded samples and supervised model refinement; otherwise revise the sampling, measurement protocol or method choice.
