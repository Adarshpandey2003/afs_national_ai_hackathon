## Measurement concept

**Probe geometry**

- For the clear whey stream, a transmission or transflection probe with a controlled optical pathlength of 1–10 mm is the candidate geometry; the pathlength must be tested because too wide a path loses signal to absorption and too narrow samples a negligible volume. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- For the opaque milk concentrate, a diffuse reflection probe with separate illumination and backscatter collection fibers is the candidate geometry; the probe is flush-mounted into a pipe or vat. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

**Location**

- The exact measurement point is unknown; for planning, assume a representative liquid access point on each stream, with the concentrate measured in a pipe or vat and the whey measured in a clear process line or a bypass/transmission cell. *(assumption)*

## Risks and uncertainty

- NIR fat/protein calibrations are matrix-specific and can be disturbed by overlapping bands and physical variation; NIR has average selectivity in the vendor comparison table. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- Reflection is sensitive to surface characteristics and fat globule size, so process changes in homogenisation or temperature can mimic composition changes. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17]

- Transmission pathlength control is critical: too wide a path loses signal to absorption; too narrow samples a negligible volume. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18]

- Contact interfaces such as ATR and reflection probes can foul and require cleaning; the sources show a sapphire window can aid cleaning but provide no dairy fouling or cleaning-in-place performance data. *(inference)* [S03 p.5]

- The vendor evidence supports feasibility testing, not validated accuracy for a new dairy installation; no dairy-specific accuracy, detection limit, or maintenance figures are given. *(inference)*

1. **Define target and sample set**: Agree target variables/ranges and success criteria; collect paired samples from both streams with the client's independent laboratory protein/fat results and process state records.

2. **Laboratory spectral screening**: On the same NIR spectrometer/light source, record NIR transmission/transflection spectra for whey and NIR diffuse reflection spectra for concentrate; optionally record MIR-ATR and transflection on the same samples. [S01 p.18; S02 p.3; S01 p.9]

3. **Build and validate single-method models**: Convert reflection to absorbance, apply MSC/SNV, build PLS models for protein/fat where relevant; validate with independent subset or segmented CV and permutation testing. [S01 p.17; S03 p.6; S03 p.13]

4. **Compare interfaces on held-out samples**: Compare transmission vs transflection vs reflection probes, and MIR-ATR, with pathlength variants on independent samples; rank by prediction error, bias and ability to follow change against client-agreed targets. [S01 p.18]

5. **Inline trial and simplification outlook**: Test the best candidate or candidates at the real measurement point with timed reference samples; if successful, identify key wavelengths for a simplified dedicated multi-wavelength sensor using LEDs or filters. [S02 p.18; S03 p.14; S01 p.9]

**Comparison:** Compare probe geometries and methods on the same representative samples, with the same NIR spectrometer/light source where possible, using independent reference values and held-out validation; choose the simplest configuration that meets client-agreed accuracy, bias and maintenance needs.

**Go / no-go:** Go/no-go is based on client-agreed numerical acceptance criteria for protein/fat error, bias, response and robustness on independent samples; no acceptance numbers are invented here.
