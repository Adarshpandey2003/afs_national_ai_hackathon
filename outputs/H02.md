## Recommendation

### NIR, stream-specific geometry (Transmission/transflection probe for clear whey; diffuse reflection probe for opaque milk concentrate): primary [S01 p.17; S01 p.18]

- NIR transmission is the preferred method for transparent or semi-transparent liquids such as skim milk, whey or permeate, and generally offers higher accuracy for low-concentration components because the light interacts with the entire sampled cross-section. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18]

- Inline NIR reflection is primarily used for opaque or highly concentrated dairy products such as yogurt, butter or whole milk concentrates, and can quantify fat and protein by converting reflectance to absorbance, applying MSC/SNV, and using PLS. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17]

- Using the same NIR spectrometer and light source for both reflection and transmission supports one analyzer with swapped probes during an interface comparison. *(inference)* [S01 p.18]

Applies if: Laboratory protein and fat results span the client's actual concentration and physical variability for each stream.; Representative sample access and hygienic process conditions are available for probe trials.

### NIR transflection (Transflection probe): comparison [S01 p.18]

- In some cases, a transflection probe can already be a combined solution working well for low-fat and high-fat containing milk products; the NIR spectrometer and light source remain the same for both reflection and transmission techniques. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18]

Applies if: A single interface is preferred and can achieve the required accuracy for both streams.; Signal quality and pathlength are suitable for both the clear whey and the opaque concentrate.

### MIR ATR (ATR probe / contact crystal): comparison [S01 p.16; S01 p.18; S01 Table 1 (p.6); S04 p.3]

- MIR ATR provides a higher level of molecular specificity than NIR and is useful for specialized milk analysis; ATR relies on an evanescent wave that penetrates only micrometres, enabling measurement of highly scattering fluids without dilution. *(sourced)* [S01 p.16; S01 p.18; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- Candidate MIR features for protein and fat are the amide I/II bands near 1548 and 1640 cm⁻¹ and the triglyceride ester carbonyl near 1740 cm⁻¹, demonstrated in model biological tissues. *(sourced)* [S04 p.3]

Applies if: NIR selectivity is judged insufficient for the required protein/fat distinction.; Contact, fouling, cleaning and fiber/crystal compatibility are confirmed for the dairy streams.

## Measurement concept

**Probe geometry**

- For the clear whey stream, use a transmission/transflection probe with a controlled pathlength of about 1–10 mm; the light passes through a defined sample layer to a detector on the opposite side. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- For the opaque milk concentrate, use a diffuse reflection probe with one set of fibers delivering light and another collecting backscattered light, flush-mounted into a pipe or vat; the optical path depth is about 0.5–1 mm. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- For the MIR ATR comparison, use an ATR probe/crystal in contact with the liquid or paste, sampling roughly 1–6 µm into the sample. *(sourced)* [S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- Milk concentrate: flush-mount the reflection probe into a pipe or vat where the flowing medium contacts the probe; the exact location and access need client confirmation. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17]

## Risks and uncertainty

- Scattering, fat globule size and surface characteristics affect NIR reflection; MSC/SNV preprocessing is needed before PLS, so physical variation may imitate composition changes. *(sourced)* [S01 p.17]

- NIR models are matrix-specific; a whey model will not transfer to milk concentrate, and each stream requires its own calibration and reference range. *(inference)* [S01 p.10; S01 p.17; S01 p.18]

- For the whey transmission path, a pathlength that is too wide loses signal to absorption and a path that is too narrow samples a negligible volume; this screening step can change feasibility. *(sourced)* [S01 p.18]

- MIR ATR is a contact crystal method; no dairy CIP/fouling data is provided in the sources, so hygienic compatibility and cleaning frequency are open risks. *(inference)* [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S02 p.6]

- All dairy-specific statements in the supplied material are vendor application examples, not validated performance for this customer; no accuracy, detection limit, response time or robustness figures are available. *(inference)* [S01 p.17; S01 p.18]
