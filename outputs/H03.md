## Risks and uncertainty

- Fermentation media are complex multicomponent mixtures where absorption, Rayleigh/Raman scattering, and fluorescence overlap, so a single method may not separate glucose or biomass from interferences. *(sourced)* [S01 p.7]

- High fluorescence sensitivity does not provide selectivity; without a defined fluorescent species or marker, a fluorescence-based glucose or viable biomass calibration would be unsupported. *(inference)* [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.16]

- Physical variation in the broth, such as turbidity, cell density, bubbles, and temperature, can mimic or mask analyte signals and invalidate NIR or UV/Vis models. *(inference)* [S01 p.7; S01 p.16]

- Raman measurements in biological media can suffer from fluorescence background, which may obscure the glucose signal. *(inference)* [S01 p.7]

- Optical density/turbidity is not a direct viable cell count; a viability definition and independent validation are required, or the biomass measurement cannot meet the requested target. *(inference)* [S01 p.16]
