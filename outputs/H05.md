### NIR (Transmission or transflection probe): comparison [S01 p.10; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

### Raman (Raman probe): comparison [S01 p.11; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

- Raman provides high selectivity for liquid mixtures containing organic and inorganic substances, but the signal is weak and fluorescence can overwhelm it for many organic compounds. *(sourced)* [S01 p.11]

**Objective:** Determine whether any optical method can separate on-specification from off-specification liquid samples against an independent reference, and identify which candidate methods or spectral ranges carry the useful signal, before selecting a method or configuration.

**Samples**

- Representative on-specification liquid samples across normal production variation.

- Process blank or clean liquid for background assessment.

**Reference measurements**

- The existing QC/reference method for the affected quality variable, with timed sampling to match spectral scans.

- Client records of off-spec episodes and any existing auxiliary measurements such as pH, conductivity, colour or turbidity.

1. **Define the problem**: Obtain client answers to priority questions q1-q3: define the affected quality variable, specification limits, suspected contaminant class and concentration range, and identify an accessible measurement point.

2. **Multi-method laboratory comparison**: Record MIR-ATR, NIR transmission/transflection and Raman spectra of on-spec and off-spec samples using the AFS multispectral platform across the 0.3-16 µm range, together with reference values; add fluorescence only if a fluorescent species is justified. [S02 p.3; S05 p.1]

3. **Identify key spectral features**: Use chemometric classification or calibration to compare each candidate method and identify which spectral ranges or bands separate the classes. [S01 p.5; S02 p.20]

4. **Select candidate method for in-line trial**: Only after step 2-3, choose the most promising method and a suitable probe geometry for the real process point; do not commit to an exact configuration before this. [S01 p.9; S02 p.20]

5. **In-line or slipstream trial against timed reference samples**: If step 4 is supported, test at the real measurement point with timed reference samples to check robustness to process variation and fouling. [S02 p.3]

**Comparison:** Compare on-spec versus off-spec classification performance and signal-to-background for each candidate method; do not compare methods as a final decision until the target variable is defined.

**Go / no-go:** Agree acceptance criteria with the client, such as classification sensitivity/specificity, detection limit for the suspected contaminant and response time. Proceed only if a method shows usable spectral contrast anchored to reference data.
