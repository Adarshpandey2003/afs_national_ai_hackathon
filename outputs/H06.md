## Feasibility plan

**Objective:** Establish whether any optical method can distinguish tumor boundary from normal tissue in the customer's intended tissue and workflow, before any clinical readiness claim.

**Samples**

- Representative customer tumor, boundary/transitional, and normal tissue specimens across the intended organ and variation.

- Aliquots of the same samples for independent histopathology and repeated optical measurements to assess repeatability.

- If combinations are later considered, the same specimens measured with all candidate methods at the same marked spots.

**Reference measurements**

- Independent histopathology or agreed surgical pathology classification of each measured spot as tumor, boundary, or normal.

- Timed or spot-matched reference labels for each optical measurement; no clinical latency or in vivo references at this stage.

1. **Define target and reference standard**: Agree tumor type, boundary definition, required decision confidence, and histopathology ground truth with the customer. This is a prerequisite before any spectral work.

2. **Laboratory screening of candidate methods**: Record full-spectrum NIR and, where justified, the adapted LED concept and/or multispectral combinations on representative ex vivo tissue samples. Compare optical contrast and reproducibility. [S01 p.10; S03 p.14]

3. **Build and validate chemometric models against histopathology**: Develop calibration or classification models on optical data referenced to histopathology. Validate with held-out samples and permutation-style tests to estimate risk of chance discrimination. [S03 p.13; S01 p.9]

4. **Assess single-method sufficiency and simplify if possible**: If a single method meets the agreed boundary criteria, test whether the key wavelengths can support a simplified LED or multisensor system. If not, test data fusion from a simultaneous multispectral probe. [S01 p.9; S03 p.14]

5. **Plan a separate in vivo study only after laboratory milestone**: Do not proceed to live surgery on this evidence. A future clinical feasibility study would require its own protocol, ethics approval, sterile probe interface, latency specification, and independent validation.

**Comparison:** Compare candidate methods on the same representative samples against histopathology: full-spectrum NIR vs adapted LED concept, and if needed combined NIR+IR/fluorescence; choose the simplest method that meets the agreed boundary discrimination criteria.

**Go / no-go:** Agree numerical acceptance criteria with the customer before testing. A go decision requires laboratory models to meet those criteria on independent specimens and a written plan for separate clinical validation; absence of such evidence is a no-go for live-surgical use.
